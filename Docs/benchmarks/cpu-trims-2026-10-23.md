# CPU trims — 2026-10-23

YouKnow 1.3.4 removes repeated work from the engine's audio path without changing a rendered sample. The baseline is `ee9fa8c` (1.3.3). This is an unreleased source change measured on a development container; no installed plug-in or Rack Extension was replaced and nothing here is a Reason-host measurement.

## Changes

One mechanism per commit, each verified bit-identical before the next.

1. **VCF solve setup** (`OtaCascade::process`). A supplied control trajectory was sanitized twice, once while planning the tableau and again at the nodes the tableau reads, and nine per-node arrays were zero-filled although a cheaper tableau never reads the skipped nodes. The nodes are sanitized once for both readers and the skipped entries are left indeterminate; every entry a tableau reads is still written first. `prepareStageNoise` chooses its reconstruction degree once per call instead of once per stage. About 3 % fewer instructions per block.
2. **FIR transports through contiguous windows.** The voice-island, chorus-island and output-summer transports (`VoiceVcaAntialias`, `ChorusRateIsland`, `OutputSummerAntialias`) keep their input and output rings twice over: every write lands at `[cursor]` and `[cursor + size]`, so the last `size` samples are one contiguous block and the reconstruction and decimation taps read it through a plain pointer instead of masking each index. The products and the four interleaved partial sums are formed in the same order as before. The voice island also builds one filter frame per internal sample and rewrites it per local interval instead of zero-filling a fresh one. About 2 % more.
3. **Inline square roots.** `std::sqrt` on a double is the C library's `sqrt`; under the Rack toolchain's `-fno-builtin` every call stays a real call. `inlineSqrt` names the builtin on GCC and Clang (the instruction, correctly rounded like libm) for the per-interval voice-VCA output-noise root, the shot-noise Cholesky pivots and the eight `sqrt(3)` unit-RMS factors the output stage recomputed every host sample. About 0.3 % more under these Rack-like flags; a toolchain that already inlines `sqrt`, as the plug-in build does, gains only the folded constants.

Circuit equations, kernels, tableaux, processing rates, latency, noise models and every random draw are unchanged.

## Audio parity

A null-test harness (`cpu-trims-2026-10-23/nulltest.cpp`) renders every factory and product preset through the product configuration — `ActiveProductFidelityProfile`, measured converter chart, PolyZoned/Cubic/RK4Single, Aging 50 %, six voices, 1x, Original timing, 64-frame callbacks — for one 3.6 s phrase (a six-note chord, two bass notes, a three-note chord under a pitch-bend sweep) after the demo renderer's 0.37 s pre-roll, and writes raw stereo float32. `compare_null.py` subtracts baseline from candidate.

- 48 kHz, Normal Circuit Rate: **all 144 presets bit-identical** ([per-preset table](cpu-trims-2026-10-23-null-test.csv); every residual column reads −999 dB, the comparison's "no difference" marker).
- 48 kHz, Max Circuit Rate: 13 presets spread across both banks, bit-identical.
- Benchmark stream hashes (FNV-1a over every output sample) equal between baseline and 1.3.4 at 48 kHz Normal (four presets and the synthetic resonant patch), 48 kHz Max, and 96 kHz Direct timing with 2x oversampling.

This is deterministic parity on GCC 13 / x86-64 with the Rack-like flags below; the arithmetic is order-preserving by construction on every platform, and the existing C++17 embedded build is what the Rack compiles.

## Considered and not shipped

The Ceres round of the same day was checked against YouKnow's topology:

- **Rational `tanh`/`exp`/`sin`/`tan` kernels.** The product VCF already uses the polynomial `PolyZoned` kernel; the remaining libm on the audio path (`pow`/`exp2` in the cutoff law, `exp`/`log` in the DCO reset and coupled mixer, `log1p`/`expm1` in the chorus clock) is about 2.6 % of the instructions, and any last-bit change is not null-testable here (below). Not applied.
- **Newton stop thresholds** (chorus follower junctions at 2e-12, coupled mixer): the same last-bit objection for at most 1–4 %. Not applied.
- **Per-sample divisions as rate coefficients:** last-bit differences, same objection. Not applied.
- **Four interleaved FIR partial sums:** already present since 1.3.0 in the transports this round touched.
- **Sign-test phase wraps:** already in place on the hot paths; the remaining `floor` calls are guarded or control-rate.
- **Skipping unused sources exactly** and **skipping shaping at zero level:** the continuously advanced states here (main-noise support filters, headphone route, BLEP correction rings) are kept running by design, and a resume would be a different realisation; not applied without the owner's ear.

**Why last-bit changes are not null-testable in YouKnow.** Fifteen factory presets set resonance to its maximum and self-oscillate. Multiplying the cutoff by one float ulp (`1 + 1.2e-7`) leaves 129 presets bit-identical or within −139 dB, but B77 and B21 diverge to −5.5 dB and −2.5 dB re the signal within 40 ms: the oscillation runs away from its twin. A change that is not bit-exact can therefore only be judged on those presets by listening or by statistics, not by a residual.

**The one candidate that was worth a listen.** Refreshing the island's collector-noise covariance factor on every fourth internal sample instead of every one (every interval still draws its own innovation; no random draw moves) saves about 6 % more instructions on the factory workload, and on 129 presets the residual is 94–140 dB below the signal; on the fifteen self-oscillating presets it is the divergence above, with whole-file RMS within 0.03 dB and the chord-section spectrum of B77 differing by up to 15.8 dB in single 5.9 Hz bins. It is left out of 1.3.4 and offered as an A–Z set (A shipping, B every fourth frame, C every eighth) with its key and patch for the owner to decide.

## Tests

The DSP test tree (`-DYOUKNOW_BUILD_PLUGIN=OFF -DBUILD_TESTING=ON`, Release, GCC 13, x86-64), run on the final tree:

- `unit` tier: 122 tests. On the first run 121 passed and `YouKnow.VoiceVcaAntialias` failed, because its frozen single-sum oracle compares the whole ring storage and still wrote only one half of the now-mirrored rings; the oracle was taught the new storage convention (it writes both halves and keeps its original masked reads) and the test passes. The batching-equivalence check it exists for is unchanged. The same tier passed 122/122 on the baseline before the change.
- `fuzz` tier: `YouKnow.DspFuzz`, passed.
- `audio` tier: `YouKnow.AudioDemos` (all 144 presets rendered twice at 4x with Exact tanh and the half-step Merson solver), passed in 3,641 s.
- The `render` tier (the editor screenshot) belongs to the plug-in build and was not run here.
- Per mechanism, before the next one was started: the 144-preset null test bit-identical and the benchmark hashes equal; the full tiers above ran once, on the final tree.

## Reproduce

```sh
# harness: Docs/benchmarks/cpu-trims-2026-10-23/
H=Docs/benchmarks/cpu-trims-2026-10-23
$H/build.sh /path/to/baseline-checkout bench /tmp/bench-baseline rack
$H/build.sh . bench /tmp/bench-candidate rack
valgrind --tool=callgrind --collect-atstart=no /tmp/bench-candidate original 48000 normal 300 2 1 0
$H/build.sh /path/to/baseline-checkout nulltest /tmp/nulltest-baseline plugin
$H/build.sh . nulltest /tmp/nulltest-candidate plugin
/tmp/nulltest-baseline /tmp/null-base original 48000 normal
/tmp/nulltest-candidate /tmp/null-cand original 48000 normal
python3 -I $H/compare_null.py /tmp/null-base /tmp/null-cand --csv /tmp/null.csv
$H/paired_timing.sh /tmp/bench-baseline /tmp/bench-candidate /tmp/timing.csv 5 300
```

`bench.cpp` includes `<valgrind/callgrind.h>` for the collection toggles (the two macros are no-ops outside valgrind; drop them if the headers are absent). `bench` takes `original|direct RATE max|normal BLOCKS [chorus 0|1|2] [factor 1|2|4] [preset index, -1 = synthetic patch]`; `nulltest` takes `OUTDIR original|direct RATE max|normal [FIRST LAST]`. `rack` builds every DSP translation unit as the Rack does (C++17, `-O3 -fno-builtin -DYOUKNOW_EMBEDDED_TARGET=1`); `plugin` builds them as the plug-in does (C++20, `-O3`). Use the same compiler for both sides and keep every run.
